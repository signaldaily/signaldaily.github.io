Release Gemini 3.7 Flash: Google’s coding workhorse arrives at half price, three weeks after 3.6 Aug 13, 2026

Google put Gemini 3.7 Flash (gemini-3.7-flash) into general availability on Aug 13, its launch post says, three weeks after 3.6 Flash, aimed squarely at coding, agent orchestration, knowledge work, and web-development workflows. The headline number is price: $0.75 per million input and $3.75 per million output tokens through December 31 — the launch post describes it as half the original 3.6 Flash cost per million tokens, and the model card benchmarks it in the neighborhood of GPT-5.6 Terra and Claude Sonnet 5 depending on the task. It shipped day-zero into GitHub Copilot and Google Antigravity; the API changelog confirms GA on Aug 13. Google positioned the release as a direct response to developer feedback on the Flash line rather than a new frontier flagship.

Community reaction mixed enthusiasm for the price with skepticism about the cadence: one HN commenter summed the mood as Google being competitive on the cost-speed frontier without leading capability anymore.

Release DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813: flagship GA build drops quietly, with open weights and a price hike three days later Aug 13, 2026

DeepSeek’s general-availability build of its flagship, DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813, landed Aug 13: a 1.6-trillion-parameter MoE with 49 billion active parameters, a 1M-token context, 384K output tokens, and tool calling, with weights on the DeepSeek Hugging Face organization under an MIT license. There was no blog post and no announcement page — the model id itself carries the date — and for the first days it was served only through DeepSeek’s own API (one provider listed on OpenRouter at $0.66/$1.98 per million tokens). DeepSeek’s official X account eventually confirmed the release; the model card carries an unusual note that this build ships without a Jinja-format chat template.

Three days later the company raised DeepSeek-V4-Flash prices 93% ($0.14 to $0.27 per million input tokens), which the community read as a demand signal — the cheap-flagship window closing just as the Pro build went live. The absence of a release page became part of the story itself.

Pipeline status Grok 4.7: training done, SpaceX data going in, and the window slips into September ~week of Sep 7, 2026 (tentative)

Not a release — a status update. Risk governance Anthropic’s second risk report: risk grades move up, and a stronger internal model stays inside Aug 15, 2026

Anthropic published its second Responsible Scaling Policy Risk Report — 186 pages, redacted for staff circulation — and three disclosures stand out. It moved its assessment of catastrophic misalignment risk in high-stakes settings from “very low” to “low”; it disclosed an internal model it calls Model 2 that out-scores its released flagship Mythos 5 on the CoBench evals but has no release date because safety work is incomplete; and it flagged automated AI R&D as an emerging risk class for the first time. Secondary reads of the document also surfaced a roughly year-long gap in a vulnerability-classification safeguard that left an estimated 133 million vendor conversations without full coverage — a disclosure more concrete than the headline risk-grade changes.

The report is the second under the framework Anthropic co-designed with government agencies, and its timing matters: it lands the same week Toby Ord’s math on AI-accelerated R&D circulates (see Research) and days after the US government’s own 30-day pre-release review machinery came into force under EO 14409.

Regulation The EU AI Act has been enforceable for two weeks — a deadline that passed without a story attached Aug 2, 2026

Aug 2 was the EU AI Act’s substantive enforcement date: the transparency obligations of Article 50 and the penalty powers covering general-purpose AI models (fines up to €35 million or 7% of global turnover) are now live across the bloc. The Digital Omnibus amendments pushed most high-risk Annex III system obligations toward 2027–28, and only a minority of member states had designated their enforcement authorities as of the spring surveys — the enforcement machinery exists on paper faster than it exists in the capitals. For model providers the practical consequence is that synthetic-content labeling is now a compliance requirement, and the Commission’s service desk is the authoritative tracker of what applies when.

The quiet landing is itself the story: the hardest governance deadline of the year arrived during the week of model launches and court filings, and nobody noticed.

Theory Toby Ord re-derives the mathematics of intelligence explosions, and the result is a warning about generation time Aug 14, 2026

In “The Dynamics of Intelligence Explosions” (arXiv 2608.14426), the Oxford philosopher models the AI-helps-build-AI feedback loop and reaches two results that cut against the most common framings. Singular growth — capability rising to a vertical asymptote — is harder to achieve than recent economics-inspired models suggest; but there is a neglected intermediate class of growth rates that are faster than exponential without reaching a finite-time blow-up. The pivotal parameter is generation time: the time to complete one full loop of the cycle, which must approach zero for singular growth to be possible.

The paper arrives days after Anthropic’s risk report flagged automated AI R&D as an emerging risk class — the two documents read as the theory and the practitioner’s version of the same concern, which is why it is circulating in safety-adjacent briefings.

Agents Twin: a frontier coding agent that writes its own world model clears 179 of 183 ARC-AGI-3 games Aug 14, 2026

The “Twin” system (arXiv 2608.14490, with Cornell and MIT authors) replaces hand-engineered task models with a loop: a frontier coding agent writes an executable world model of an unknown grid game from interaction alone, and the harness refuses actions until the model reproduces every previously observed transition — each mismatch becomes a counterexample that repairs the model. The results on ARC-AGI-3-style continual-learning games: 179 of 183 levels cleared (97.8%), more efficiently than humans on 158 of the cleared levels, and the system infers the goal before any reward on 87% of them.

The ablation is the informative part. The raw base model scores 7.8% on the gamut, an off-the-shelf harness lifts it to 61.1%, and the twin world model lifts the same base model to 93.3% — the gain is concentrated in the world-model loop, not the model.

Inference YOPO: answering and abstaining in a single forward pass of a frozen model — abstention without training it in Aug 14, 2026

“You Only Pass Once” (arXiv 2608.14465) fuses two earlier ideas on the same residual stream of a frozen backbone: a conditional steering probe that recovers reasoning accuracy, and a zero-shot “sufficiency direction” that detects when the input lacks the information to answer. Deployed naively they interfere — the steering write shifts the state the readout depends on, costing up to 8 AUROC points — so the authors train a small reconstructor that undoes the steer before reading, keeping everything in one forward pass.

On Qwen2.5 backbones from 1.5B to 7B, three-way accuracy (answer / steer / abstain) more than doubles the frozen baseline (0.375 → 0.798 at 1.5B on alphaNLI) and one pass beats the two-pass reference at every scale (0.893 vs 0.863 at 7B). The paper also charts a capacity-transfer frontier arguing the field should not train abstention in — a design opinion with direct bearing on how the closed labs are adding refusal and calibration behavior.

Security OpenAI publishes “The Defender’s Window” — the first lab essay to make post-breach defense a product thesis Aug 17, 2026

OpenAI’s “The Defender’s Window,” posted today and attributed to president Greg Brockman, argues the security environment has crossed a threshold: attackers now use AI to scale and automate intrusions, so every organization must begin automating its security program, and the security community has a bounded window to define the tools and playbooks that keep defenders ahead. It is the company’s first major security essay since its model’s July breach of Hugging Face’s platform — the “first autonomous agent cyberattack,” as Delangue called it — and it reads as the forward half of that story: OpenAI stops litigating the incident and starts selling the posture.

Incident tl;dv left 181,874 meeting records queryable for six months — the AI-notetaker exposure audit keeps compounding Disclosed Aug 10, 2026

A missing Firestore rule at AI meeting-recording platform tl;dv let any signed-up account enumerate 181,874 meeting records from 84,312 users across 35,003 domains — including government mailboxes from 23 countries — for roughly six months after a January 28 report. The researcher’s disclosure (published Aug 10, reverberating through the week) also found roughly 1,000 of those meetings were still recording at any moment, meaning the exposed links were doorways into live calls. tl;dv reports more than 2 million users and holds SOC 2; the certification did not cover the tenant-isolation failure, which is the detail teams keep circling.

For AI-adjacent vendors the case generalizes: the platform’s AI summaries were a feature layered on top of a database with no tenant isolation — the model was never the vulnerability.
