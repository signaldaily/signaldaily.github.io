The day the traces spoke

A Tübingen–Max Planck team showed that three frontier labs encrypt hidden reasoning with one shared key per provider — and decoded 315,320 of those traces from public agent logs. What the attack actually does, what it doesn't, and why it lands the same week Anthropic crossed into profit.

Field Report · 01 The ledeone key, three labsAug 16, 2026

Encrypted reasoning traces were supposed to be the part of the response nobody could read. OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google each wrap their models' hidden chain-of-thought in ciphertext before sending it through the API, partly to protect against distillation and partly to keep private reasoning private. This week, researchers at the ELLIS Institute Tübingen and the Max Planck Institute published a demonstration that the wrapping is shared: each provider uses a single global encryption key for every session, every user, and every model it serves (arXiv 2608.09867).

That one design choice collapses the protection. Because the key never rotates, a compatible decoder model from the same provider can transcribe another model's encrypted reasoning in plaintext — what the authors call a decryption jailbreak. Working from 6,708 public agent trajectory logs, the team decoded 315,320 reasoning blocks and recovered 182 live credentials, including API keys that had been reasoned over in the hidden layer. The attack spans four vectors: circumventing anti-distillation defenses, extracting private data at scale, reading the reasoning of a stronger model through a weaker sibling, and reconstructing traces from traffic that was never meant to be legible.

Takeaway · The vulnerability is not a broken cipher — it is a key-management decision, and the same key that hides reasoning also leaks it.

The reaction from people who actually run agent infrastructure was less surprise than resignation. Security researchers had flagged the replay design months earlier — Matthew Green at Johns Hopkins pointed at the construction in May, and according to the paper's disclosure timeline the labs initially saw no security implication until a working attack existed. Providers have now deployed mitigations that break the main exploit path, but neither per-session key isolation nor an independent audit has been announced by any of the three.

The deeper consequence sits with the open-weight ecosystem. If proprietary reasoning can be read at scale, the anti-distillation moat that justified hiding the traces gets weaker — and the argument for releasing reasoning openly, on open models, gets stronger by symmetry. The paper's authors tested six open-weight reasoning models (Kimi K3, Kimi K2.6, Kimi K2.5, GLM-5.2, DeepSeek-V3.1, Inkling) against the same 90-problem suite, which means the comparison baseline between closed traces and open ones is now public too.

Field Report · 02 The counterweightAnthropic's quarterAug 16, 2026

Against that backdrop, Anthropic's reported quarter reads differently. The company told investors it expects $10.9 billion in Q2 2026 revenue — up 130% from $4.8 billion in Q1 — with a $559 million operating profit, the first for any frontier lab at this scale. The mechanism underneath the headline is the cost line: compute spend fell from 71 cents to 56 cents per revenue dollar, driven by serving-efficiency work ahead of an expected October listing. Both figures are reported rather than audited, and at least one account notes a compute-ramp discount that may have flattered the quarter, so treat the profit as a signal rather than a verdict.

The juxtaposition is the story. The same week one of the three labs in the trace paper reported its first profit, the cryptographic assumption protecting all three labs' reasoning was shown to be broken by design. Neither event cancels the other — but anyone building on these APIs now has two new facts to price in at once: the economics of frontier serving can work, and the secrecy of frontier reasoning currently doesn't.

Field Report · 03 The rest of the day, briefly03 The rest of the day, brieflyAug 16, 2026

Elsewhere, Stripe finalized its acquisition of OpenRouter for more than $7 billion, per TechCrunch's report on the Bloomberg story — payments infrastructure absorbing the model-routing layer at roughly seven times OpenRouter's May valuation. Z.ai's GLM-5.3 launched on a 743B-parameter base with leading CyberGym scores but staged, not shipped, open weights. Qwen passed 3 billion cumulative downloads, topping the open-model charts. Google open-sourced HEIR, a compiler turning pretrained models into encrypted-inference programs — a different, deliberate approach to the privacy problem the trace paper attacked. And Geoffrey Hinton told interviewers that mass unemployment is coming and that the executives betting on replacement know it — a voice item that, like the Anthropic numbers, lands harder in a week when the economics visibly turned.

Nothing else cleared the bar today. The full trace paper is worth reading directly — the appendix reproduces extracted chains-of-thought verbatim, which is the closest the public has come to reading a frontier model's mind.
