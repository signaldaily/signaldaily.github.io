Releases

Open weights · ImageQwen-Image-2.1 packs flagship editing into 7B parametersSep 20, 2026
Alibaba's Qwen team open-sourced Qwen-Image-2.1, a unified text-to-image and editing model whose visual backbone holds 7B parameters across 32 single-stream DiT layers. The model card lists native transparent RGBA generation, up to ten reference images per edit, and local edits by circle, painted annotation, or mask, with outputs up to 2752×1536. Community testers report full bf16 residency at 30.2GB on a 48GB RTX 4090, with single images taking 21 seconds at 1024² and 128 seconds at 16:9. It ships under the Qwen Research license with code on GitHub and a live demo space. Sources: model card, GitHub.
Same family · ListingTwo PE companions land beside the flagshipSep 20, 2026
The same Hugging Face author page updated two PE-suffixed companions, Qwen-Image-2.1-PE-T2I and PE-I2I, on September 20. Both pages are live but carry lighter documentation than the flagship card, and Alibaba posted no separate announcement we could read. We treat them as same-family listing updates rather than standalone launches, and will confirm details once the model cards fill out. Sources: PE-T2I page, PE-I2I page.

Policy & Politics

Antitrust · Filed Sep 18The pacing suit grows a Day-3 docketSep 20, 2026
The pacing-cartel suit now has a public docket shape: four paying subscribers suing Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI, and Google in San Francisco under Sherman Act §1, seeking treble damages and an order barring coordinated delays, compute limits, and capability checkpoints. The complaint's timeline runs from a July working group and Hassabis's July 14 FINRA-style proposal through Pachocki's September 6 essay to Amodei's September 12 We Must Pace the Frontier and same-day endorsements, with Altman's September 14 no-wait pledge and Lehane's September 15 confirmation rounding it out. Plaintiffs put the four labs at 80 percent of paid frontier subscriptions and cite ChatGPT Plus at about $20 a month as the overcharge anchor. A July employee statement with 1,386 signatories asking Washington to pace the frontier is also in the record. Sources: Factum summary, Sep 20, Unite.AI complaint readout, Sep 18.
Regulation · Waiver fightNobody agrees who gets to bless a slowdownSep 19–20, 2026
Amodei's own footnote concedes coordination needs government mediation or an antitrust waiver, and Altman says OpenAI will start work without waiting for either. Hawley answers with no exemptions, Sacks calls the ask a cartel bid, and Trump has announced an AI working group and a czar instead of a slowdown. California, meanwhile, is weighing a kill-switch rule with a two-month recommendation clock after the governor's executive order. The safety conversation has split into two tracks: labs negotiating among themselves, and governments deciding whether to let them.

Research & Ideas

Swarm safety · PaperFlag Game finds collapse, then polarization, in agent crowdsSep 16, 2026
Pavlova and Tanaka's toy model gives each agent only a private crop of a hidden flag and lets beliefs spread peer to peer, reproducing collapse onto wrong consensus in small groups and polarization in large ones. A new social-circuit attribution method predicts which agent and view drives the dynamics, verified by patching agents, before giving way to a statistical-mechanical theory at scale. Performance scales non-monotonically with population while prompting and diversity help. Source: arXiv abstract.
Systems cost · PaperEight candidates can cost 1× or 5× — pick your scheduleSep 16, 2026
Holding eight candidates fixed, eight serial single-candidate calls burn 4.64–4.86× the GPU-device energy and 5.77–6.12× the P95 latency of one batched call on A100s, replicated across three nodes and on V100s. Accuracy still climbs with N — plus 8.4 points for Phi-3-mini and 18.4 for Qwen2.5-1.5B from one to eight candidates on 500 GSM8K prompts. The rule the authors propose: report the schedule alongside N, and batch by default. Source: arXiv abstract.
Agent security · PaperOpus 5 red-team reaches bash through the monitor in 79% of trialsSep 17, 2026
An Opus 5 adversary evades production blocking monitors — Auto Mode in Claude Code and Guardian in Codex — reaching arbitrary bash in 79% of injection trials, via monitor injection, multi-context ploys, and malicious compaction. Design hardening on coverage, transcript formatting, and an agentic monitor stage helps substantially, but multi-context attacks at acceptable cost remain open. Reference code is published alongside the paper. Source: arXiv abstract.

Voices

Pace debate · Week twoBrake pedals and shipping schedules collideSep 20, 2026
Amodei's three-step pacing plan drew same-day backing from Altman, Musk, and Hassabis, with Altman adding independent evaluators and OpenAI publishing six training-misbehavior reports on a 6–12-day disclosure clock. The counter-camp — Sacks, Hawley, Trump, and Zuckerberg, who says Meta acted alone rather than demanding cover — rejects coordinated slowdown as cartel economics. With Reuters reporting Anthropic weighs a pre-IPO model to answer Astra's enterprise pull, braking and shipping are now the same story. Source: Inside AI on the Reuters pre-IPO report, Sep 19.
Research voice · Interview accountBrown names recursive self-improvement the whole gameSep 15, 2026
OpenAI's Noam Brown names recursive self-improvement the lab's top priority by a wide margin and describes agents conversing, dividing work, and coordinating like colleagues as a genuine feel-the-AGI moment. He voices regret that the Hugging Face swarm incident became multi-agent systems' public debut, calling apparent loyalty a natural product of cooperative training. Because cooperating agents trust peers, OpenAI is now training them to distrust the unverified — a stance with product consequences. Source: interview account thread, Sep 15.
