Frontier · Code + knowledgeGrok 4.7 ships Sep 21 as a price-performance playSep 21, 2026
SpaceXAI's Grok 4.7 arrived September 21, nine days after the September 12 date Elon Musk set on September 2, with Musk attributing the slip to reinforcement learning that penalized long answers too aggressively and left the model quitting hard tasks early. The launch post prices it identically to Grok 4.6 at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens, adding a fast variant at twice the speed for twice the price, and reports CursorBench 4.0 at 46.3% against 40.4% for 4.6, 41.7% for GPT-5.6 Sol Max, and 51.8% for Claude Fable 5.1 Max. The same table claims EEBench at 64.0%, Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 38.0% versus 20.3% for its predecessor, AA Briefcase at 1657 versus 1546, and a GDPval Elo of 1695 behind Fable 5.1's 1735, with safety numbers of 3.3% risky cyber pass-through on HackerBench and 62.4% on LatchBio biosafety. It is available in Cursor and Grok Build, through the Grok API, routers, and clouds — a delay saga that ended in undercutting, not a coronation. Sources: xAI launch post, Sep 21, delay analysis, Sep 12.

Safety standards · WashingtonOpenAI asks Washington to lead a global safety-standards pushSep 22, 2026
OpenAI is urging the US government to lead a global effort to create AI safety standards, the subject of a September 22 print edition item, days after Sam Altman posted that the company welcomes a federal framework with consistent safety requirements for frontier labs. Altman framed pacing as deliberate rather than a halt, warning that unchecked pressure could cost control of the future to AI or concentrate power around one person or company, and adding that no amount of competitive pressure should justify recklessness. The move lands ten days after Dario Amodei's September 12 slowdown essay drew same-day backing from Altman, Musk, and Hassabis. Sources: Wall Street Journal print edition, Sep 22 (direct link dropped — the article page returns 401 to automated checks), CNBC, Sep 14.
Legislation · London + BrusselsUK committee demands an AI Bill and a statutory overseer~week of Sep 14, 2026
The UK Joint Committee on Human Rights has called for a dedicated AI Bill, arguing the existing framework cannot handle AI supply-chain complexity or development speed, with acute risks for minoritised communities and privacy. Its prescription is a single independent oversight body on a statutory footing, acting as the central point for concerns, with powers to set codes of practice, impose transparency requirements, and sanction wrongdoing. The backdrop is enforcement season: the European Commission began enforcing AI Act transparency duties on August 2, requiring AI-interaction disclosure, deepfake labelling, and machine-readable provenance marks. Sources: Joint Committee on Human Rights report (official page blocked automated verification with a 403, so no direct link; details via its indexed official summary), European Commission, Jul 31.

Research & Ideas

Robotics · SafetySafeHarness teaches robot coding agents to respect obstaclesSep 17, 2026
Coding agents that write robot controllers as programs complete tasks while colliding with obstacles they perceive and were told to avoid, because the constraint never becomes a planning priority and failed routes are never replanned. SafeHarness splits manipulation into a verified, replanned waypoint route over bounding boxes plus contact positions chosen to respect the same constraint, reaching 71.9% task success with 87.5% collision avoidance — gains of 6.5 and 27.0 points over prior best, and multiples of 2.3 and 1.5 over the unaided agent. The sharp diagnosis is what matters: perception and instruction were never the fault, prioritization was. Sources: arXiv 2609.20822, Sep 17. Deep read on the Arxiv desk.
Multi-agent · ScalingTalking teams match four times their number on ARC-AGI-3Sep 17, 2026
Agents with no assigned roles sharing breakthroughs through a common directory overturn mixed prior results on collaboration: a team of k communicating agents matches the success rate of 4k independent agents on ARC-AGI-3, with the edge compounding as teams grow. The effect crosses from efficiency into capability, with teams reliably solving tasks no lone agent solves, and transfers to research work where communicating teams beat the best-known polyomino packing score and the best-known human MNIST compression solution. Sources: arXiv 2609.21032, Sep 17. Deep read on the Arxiv desk.
Reasoning · Test-time trainingRule embeddings first: cleaner ARC reasoning from 0.01% of weightsSep 18, 2026
A two-step test-time protocol for Vision ARC tunes only the transformation-rule embedding first, freezes it, then tunes the backbone — and the embedding step touches under a hundredth of a percent of parameters. Across ARC-AGI-1, ConceptARC, Mini-ARC, and two controlled known-rule sets, the split beats joint training on final scores while producing embeddings that align with true rules, retrieve better, and linearly expose known rules. Sources: arXiv 2609.21181, Sep 18. Deep read on the Arxiv desk.
