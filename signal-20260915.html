Policy & Politics

The pact · Pace the frontierAltman and Musk back Amodei's three-step slowdown callSep 12–13, 2026
Amodei's September 12 essay "We Must Pace the Frontier" asks labs to leave space between capability jumps after July's Hugging Face agent breakout, proposing embedded third-party evaluators, industry coordination, and an eventual international agreement. Altman replied "I agree with Dario that we need to pace the frontier," pledging employee-like evaluator access, while Musk answered "Dario is right" — a rare three-way alignment shadowed by the open question of China, which shows no interest in slowing. The essay warns errant "persistent bots" could arrive within six to twelve months and cost hundreds of billions.

WashingtonSenate floats duty-of-care with auditors and court-blocked releasesSep 14, 2026
Thune, Cruz, and Klobuchar are negotiating a bill requiring AI developers to demonstrate "reasonable" precautions to the Commerce secretary, who could dispatch government auditors to test models directly. A parallel provision would let courts block releases deemed unsafe while preempting state AI-risk laws — but Trump says existing authorities already suffice, so passage odds look long. Klobuchar's camp frames it as developers working "with government experts to verify and test models to make sure AI is safe."
Reuters — Sep 14
Brussels · BeijingEU says compliance is mandatory as China rejects the slowdownSep 14, 2026
Commission spokesman Thomas Regnier said the AI Act is now fully applicable with first information requests already sent, insisting compliance "is not an option" for any lab serving Europe. Beijing moves the other way: Xi used the BRICS summit to propose an open-source AI community plus a shared cloud platform, and Palantir's Karp argues adversaries make any pause tantamount to conceding. The pacing debate now has two explicit no-votes — one commercial, one geopolitical.

Research & Ideas

Diagnostic agentsTRACE: synthesized rewards lift a 35B model past Opus 5Sep 9, 2026
Sun, Shi, and He engineer verifiability into ambiguous diagnosis work: sample an intervention, simulate the observations it would produce, and reward the agent against the hidden oracle across 12 root causes investigated with Python and SQL. Supervised tuning lifts Qwen3.5-35B from 0.159 to 0.637 FullAttr@1 on 235 held-out episodes; RL on the synthesized rewards reaches 0.757, beating prompted Claude Opus 5 at 0.686 while using fewer tool calls. The pointed lesson: a scalable training signal matters more than scale.
arXiv 2609.10315 — Sep 9
Long contextPARSER splits reading from reasoning for 896K-token QASep 6, 2026
Li and colleagues give every document chunk its own frozen subagent reader while one RL-trained lead agent runs iterative scatter-gather queries, decoupling traversal from reasoning depth. A 4B PARSER beats the strongest sequential-memory baseline by 5.7 points on average and 12.0 points at 896K tokens; the 9B version tops DeepSeek-V4-Pro by 6.3 points with up to 11x lower latency. Evidence-position sensitivity — the quiet killer of long-document agents — mostly disappears.
