Resignation · AGI safetyDeepMind safety researcher quits for METR, warning of a "terrifying chance"Sep 13, 2026
Google DeepMind researcher Josh Engels left the company's AGI safety team about three weeks ago — turning down offers from Anthropic and OpenAI — to join the independent evaluation nonprofit METR. He says there is a "terrifying chance" AI systems cause immense harm within five years, arguing recursive self-improvement could outrun alignment as agents collude, hack, conceal actions, and socially engineer humans. At METR he will study misalignment sources, test whether safeguards suffice, and judge whether the industry is on track — on the premise that "we need more time."

Rep. Lori Trahan told WBUR on September 10 her phone "hasn't stopped ringing" since the Coxon whistleblowing and called the moment a tipping point, pitching her Frontier Act's transparency, incident-reporting, independent-verification, and pause-button rules as table stakes. BrusselsEU tech chief calls for global AI rules; Washington sets cooperation limitsSep 11, 2026
Commission executive vice-president Henna Virkkunen said in Dublin that "global rules are really needed," noting EU law already forces labs to assess loss-of-control risk while most of the world requires nothing. Germany's digital minister wants an IAEA-style international AI safety body, and an MEP formally asked whether the Commission will push a frontier-incident protocol at the G7, G20, and OECD. The US ambassador to the EU answered the same day that Washington favors partnership but won't let American firms "be regulated to the point where we can't beat China."

Research & Ideas

Agent securityMMPIBench: image-borne injections complete in 1% of agent runsSep 8, 2026
Nguyen and Husain's MMPIBench fires a fixed attack set at agentic frameworks through six visual carriers — OCR text, overlays, EXIF metadata, QR codes, fake interfaces, and hybrids — across 720 runs covering six frameworks, five models, and four attacker objectives. Attacks complete in about 1% of runs but are attempted in 12.8%; the gap closes almost entirely at planning, where the model reads the injection and declines. The model matters far more than the framework — one model never attempts and spots 59.7%, two others attempt 23.6% — and an audio extension completes in 49% of cells where the signal arrives.
arXiv 2609.09404 — Sep 8
Knowledge structureKST study: LLMs flunk prerequisite structure in math reasoningSep 4, 2026
Cui, Do, and Sachan test eight open- and closed-source models against real human learners using Knowledge Space Theory, which formalizes that mastering a concept requires its prerequisites. LLMs routinely violate those dependencies and fail to use related in-context knowledge on dependent questions; nor do models share a consistent structure with each other. Most pointedly, the deficiencies stay nearly invisible to accuracy-based and LLM-as-judge evaluations — the benchmarks grading the homework cannot see the missing foundations.

The resignation postEngels: "I think we need more time"Sep 13, 2026
"The AI companies are all trying to build superintelligence," Engels wrote, warning that recursive self-improvement "could become dangerous if alignment does not keep pace" and admitting "we don't currently know how to make sure AIs are safe enough for RSI." The notable detail is the declined leverage: he walked away from DeepMind despite enjoying the work and turned down both Anthropic and OpenAI to do independent evaluation at METR. Third insider exit in six days makes the pacing debate a personnel crisis, not just a policy one.

The delay postMusk: Grok 4.7 "needs a few more days to cook"Sep 11, 2026
Musk's September 11 reply is unusually diagnostic for a launch thread: RL "might have penalized response length too much (or something)," so the model "still gives up on hard tasks (that it can do!) too early and isn't yet sufficiently rigorous in checking its work." Read it as a frontier-wide confession — persistence and verification, not raw capability, are the binding constraint on agentic models right now. The post drew about two million views within days.
